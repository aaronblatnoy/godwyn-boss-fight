Godwyn X-slash v2 — final self-critique

Selected revision: **round 4**. `godwyn_xslash_v2.mp4` is one continuous three-quarter shot: 640 × 640, 90 frames, 30 fps, 3.000 seconds, encoded with libx264 and yuv420p. The editable scene is `models/astra_xslash_v2_wip.blend`, action `Astra_Godwyn_XSlash_V2`. Blender 5.2.1 rendered with Metal and `BLENDER_EEVEE`. The clip's timing is in the animation itself; the video has no speed ramps or editing cuts.

The result reads substantially better than v1: a deliberate raised tell, a sudden descending cut, an outward return into the opposite cut, and an exposed low recovery. I consider it a successful motion WIP. I would **not** describe the complete asset as shipped Elden Ring quality: the inherited sleeve/cape binding and rigid hand shapes still limit the finish.

- **Timing shape — PASS.** Frames 1–36 occupy 40% of the clip and build a slow coil with a held sword. The active cuts are F37–42 and F54–59: five frame intervals / 167 ms apiece. The opened cut sheets show large blade displacement over two-frame samples, with native blade blur during each strike. Average measured tip speed rises from 1.42 m/s in anticipation to 34.95 and 35.51 m/s in the cuts; peaks are 48.36 and 46.13 m/s. The first follow-through connects to the second wind-up over F43–53. This connector is a brisk flourish, averaging 11.96 m/s, rather than another long tell. F60–90 leaves approximately one second for deceleration, low recovery, and settling. Heavy recovery averages 3.34 m/s; the last settling interval averages 0.58 m/s. The contrast is visible in the renders, not merely implied by the timestamps.

- **Arcs — PASS, with a camera qualification.** The sword rotates through two oblique circular planes while the hand follows its own curved path. The measured three-dimensional tip paths travel 5.83 and 5.92 m, compared with straight endpoint distances of 4.45 and 4.35 m. Their maximum departures from those chords are 1.59 and 1.71 m. They cross near the upper torso in the front projection; the three-quarter diagnostics expose the forward bow of each sweep. The front projection is deliberately flatter and should not be mistaken for proof of a straight 3D trace. A transported blade orientation removes the wrist roll caused by the earlier global up-vector. The F45–53 connector matches incoming and outgoing angular tangents. Follow-through overshoots the cut endpoints before returning. All 302,379 rig keys use Bezier interpolation, sampled at quarter-frame spacing; none use linear interpolation. The movie uses native 0.55-frame shutter blur plus a faint 0.7-frame ribbon sampled from the actual blade, not persistent decorative X lines.

- **Full-body flow — PASS, with an IK limitation.** The opened F31–41 and F48–58 sequences show the chest turning while the weapon remains loaded, followed by the arm and blade. The first release's measured world-rotation peaks progress through hips at F34.25, lower spine F34.50, middle spine F34.75, upper spine F35.25, shoulder F35.75, upper arm F39.00, and hand F39.50. The second progresses from hips F51.25 through shoulder F52.50, upper arm F53.75, and hand F56.25. The torso leans into contact, the free arm counterbalances, and the first finish keeps moving into the next coil. This is not a perfectly ordered FK cascade at every joint: the analytic elbow solve overlaps the upper-arm motion, and its first velocity peak occurs half a frame earlier. The long robe also conceals some pelvic motion from the front. Those limitations remain visible; the major body-to-blade lead is nevertheless clear, especially from three-quarter view.

- **Weight — PASS.** The left ankle travels 49 cm forward and 9 cm outward, lifts approximately 13 cm, and lands at the authored first contact on F40. The pelvis drops 13.5 cm between F34 and F40, rebounds into the connector, and sinks again at the second contact on F57. Both ankle targets stay planted after F40; maximum measured drift is below 0.01 mm. The visual check shows a supported stance and bent knees rather than a floating root. Recovery rises slightly above the final pelvis level at F79, dips at F84, and eases to rest at F90. The settling bounce is small rather than a repeated bob. The robe partially obscures the planting foot, so the three-quarter view carries this criterion better than the front.

- **Secondary motion — PASS for keyed overlap; PARTIAL for finished cloth appearance.** All 97 existing secondary bones are animated: 64 robe, 21 cape, and 12 hair bones. Roots lag the body by three frames; each successive child adds two frames. Direction-change pulses travel down the chains, with tapered amplitudes and a damped recovery. The cape hem visibly continues after the torso reverses, then settles; hair overlap is subtler at this framing. The final envelope brings every secondary chain to rest by F90. There is no cloth or hair simulation. The broad cape still bends like a skinned mesh, the raised-arm fabric stretches, and some hem folds bunch rather than slide naturally. Those are genuine remaining quality limits, not a production cloth pass.
